    A wide-format historical artifact and analysis illustration (16:9 aspect ratio) presenting Paul Revere's famous engraving of the Boston Massacre (March 5, 1770) — styled as a US history textbook illustration that shows the engraving as a primary source artifact while teaching students to read it as deliberate political propaganda, comparing it with historical evidence about what actually occurred.

    COMPOSITION — PRIMARY SOURCE ANALYSIS FORMAT: The image is structured as a split documentary presentation: Revere's engraving on the left half, a contextual analysis panel on the right half — the format that teaches primary source analysis as a skill.

    LEFT HALF — REVERE'S ENGRAVING (THE ARTIFACT): A high-quality faithful reproduction of Paul Revere's engraving "The Bloody Massacre perpetrated in King-Street Boston on March 5th 1770 by a party of the 29th REGt." — originally copied (with some modifications) from Henry Pelham's earlier drawing:

    THE ENGRAVING ITSELF: - The scene takes place on King Street (now State Street), Boston, at night - On the LEFT of the engraving: a line of eight British soldiers in bright red coats, standing in a rigid disciplined firing line, their muskets all aimed simultaneously at the colonists — as Revere drew it, they fire in unison on command, suggesting premeditated murder - A British officer on the right side of the soldier line, sword raised, apparently ordering the volley — historically questionable, as Captain Thomas Preston likely did NOT give a direct fire order (his trial acquitted him) - On the RIGHT of the engraving: colonists falling dead and wounded in the snow; blood pooling dramatically on the cobblestones; three figures dead, others fleeing - Crispus Attucks (a mixed-race man of African and Native American descent, the first person killed) is visible falling in the foreground — though Revere's engraving does not specifically identify him - In the background: a building labeled "Butcher's Hall" above the customs house (a deliberate Revere addition — the building was the Custom House, not a butcher shop; he added the label to mock the British customs officials) - The sky shows a full moon and the steeple of the Old State House (Town House) - The inscription at bottom: "Engrav'd Printed & Sold by Paul Revere BOSTON"

    ENGRAVING STYLE: - Reproduced in the original copperplate engraving aesthetic: fine parallel line hatching for shading, clean outline work, limited gray-scale depth, the visual language of colonial American printmaking - The paper is aged cream with slight foxing at the margins; the ink is rich black with warm brown undertones of age

    RIGHT HALF — PRIMARY SOURCE ANALYSIS PANEL: Dark slate background, organized as a museum/textbook analysis exhibit:

    HEADER: "PRIMARY SOURCE ANALYSIS: Propaganda vs. Evidence"

    COLUMN 1 — WHAT REVERE SHOWS: (Bullet points in white text) - British soldiers in a perfect disciplined firing line - Simultaneous volley on command - Passive, unarmed colonists - Officer with raised sword ordering the massacre - Building labeled "Butcher's Hall" - Snow and blood — nighttime atrocity

    COLUMN 2 — WHAT THE EVIDENCE SHOWS: (Bullet points in contrasting amber text) - The soldiers were surrounded and pelted with ice, oyster shells, and debris by an angry crowd of 50–400 people (accounts vary widely) - Soldiers fired without a clear unified order; some fired and some did not - Crispus Attucks, the first killed, was part of the crowd confronting the soldiers - Captain Thomas Preston was acquitted at trial; most soldiers were acquitted (defended by John Adams, a Patriot) - 5 colonists killed, 6 wounded — a tragedy, but not an unprovoked military massacre - Contemporary witness accounts describe a chaotic, ambiguous confrontation

    SMALL PORTRAIT INSETS: - Paul Revere (c. 1770): the silversmith-turned-propagandist, about 35 years old, in a plain craftsman's coat; alert, practical expression - Crispus Attucks: a representation of the first person killed — a large, powerful man of African and Wampanoag descent, approximately 47 years old; the first martyr of the American Revolution

    BOTTOM CAPTION (full width): "Paul Revere published this engraving within three weeks of the incident — before any trial had occurred. It became colonial America's most effective piece of revolutionary propaganda. John Adams, who defended the British soldiers and won their acquittal, called the crowd 'a motley rabble of saucy boys, Negroes and molattoes, Irish teagues and outlandish Jack tars.' History is always told from a perspective."

    LIGHTING: - The engraving: evenly lit, museum-display quality — every detail readable - The analysis panel: dark background with white and amber text, small accent lighting - The split between the two halves is a clean vertical line, slightly visible

    TEXTBOOK ILLUSTRATION STYLE: Archival primary source analysis presentation — teaching students to read images critically. The engraving is reproduced with period-accurate printmaking aesthetics. The analysis panel uses clean contemporary museum typography. No melodrama; the goal is analytical thinking.
