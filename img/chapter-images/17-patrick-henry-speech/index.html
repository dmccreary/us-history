    A dramatic, wide-format historical illustration (16:9 aspect ratio) depicting Patrick Henry delivering his famous "Give me liberty, or give me death!" speech at the Second Virginia Convention, St. John's Church, Richmond, Virginia, on March 23, 1775 — styled as a historically grounded US history textbook illustration that conveys the electrifying power of revolutionary rhetoric while acknowledging what historians know and don't know about this moment.

    COMPOSITION — THE CHURCH INTERIOR: The scene takes place inside St. John's Church, Richmond, Virginia — a simple, modest Anglican church built in 1741, used because no building in Richmond was large enough to hold the delegates. The viewpoint is from slightly above and behind the delegates, looking toward Henry at the front of the church — a slightly theatrical angle that places the viewer among the audience witnessing the speech.

    THE CHURCH INTERIOR: - Small, plain colonial Anglican church interior: whitewashed plaster walls, box pews (enclosed wooden pew boxes with small doors, typical of the era), a simple wood-railed gallery (balcony) around three walls, plain rectangular windows letting in March afternoon light, wide-plank pine floor - The church is packed: 120 delegates filling every pew and standing along the walls; additional figures visible in the gallery above; several people visible through the open windows from outside, straining to hear - The atmosphere is tense — some delegates lean forward; others have visible expressions of alarm, excitement, or deep concentration; this is not a polite meeting but a moment of historical weight

    PATRICK HENRY — THE SPEAKER (CENTER-FOREGROUND): - Henry stands at the front of the church, visible from mid-chest upward, turned slightly so the viewer sees him in three-quarter profile — addressing the delegates while the viewer can see both Henry's face and the crowd's reaction - Age: 38 years old in March 1775; lean, angular face; dark hair; intense, deep-set eyes that contemporaries described as electrifying; a prominent nose; firmly set jaw - Clothing: a plain dark wool coat and breeches, white linen stock at his throat — Henry was known for deliberate plainness in dress to identify with ordinary Virginia farmers rather than tidewater aristocracy; no powdered wig - His right arm is raised dramatically — the gesture that painters and illustrators have traditionally associated with the climactic moment; his left hand grips the back of the pew before him for physical emphasis - His expression: fierce, passionate conviction — not performative anger but genuine urgency; contemporaries described his eyes as nearly supernatural in their intensity at his oratorical peaks - His posture conveys that he is building to the famous climax — "Give me liberty, or give me death!" — his entire body engaged in the act of persuasion

    KEY FIGURES IN THE AUDIENCE: Several historically identifiable figures are visible in the front pews: - George Washington: tall, erect, in a blue Virginia militia colonel's coat, listening with characteristic stillness and a slight forward lean — he was present - Thomas Jefferson: lean, red-haired, younger-looking at 31, slightly separate from the crowd, watching with the careful attention of a man memorizing what he is hearing - Richard Henry Lee: visible nearby, nodding - Peyton Randolph (the Convention president): at a table facing Henry, his expression showing deep concern — the older, more conservative Virginia gentry were alarmed by Henry's radical position - Edmund Pendleton: visible at Randolph's side, expression of visible unease — he opposed Henry's resolution

    LIGHTING AND ATMOSPHERE: - Late March afternoon light streaming through the plain rectangular windows, casting long warm rectangles of light across the congregation - Henry is caught in one of these shafts of afternoon light — not a supernatural glow, but the natural positioning of a speaker near the front windows - The overall light is warm but tense — spring light, but a cold war spring; the air feels charged

    HISTORICAL ACCURACY NOTES: - No authentic transcript of the speech exists — it was reconstructed decades later by William Wirt from the memories of eyewitnesses (principally St. George Tucker) — the famous final line may not be the exact words Henry used - The phrase "Liberty or Death" resonated partly because it was borrowed from a popular Scottish ballad — Henry may have been quoting something his audience would recognize - The church still stands (St. John's Church is a historic site in Richmond today)

    OPTIONAL CAPTION (lower right, dark panel, white serif text): "March 23, 1775, St. John's Church, Richmond, Virginia. Patrick Henry's final words, as reconstructed by William Wirt in 1817 from eyewitness accounts: 'I know not what course others may take; but as for me, give me liberty, or give me death!' No contemporary transcript of the speech survives."

Rothermel's 19th-century American history canvases — rich detail, dynamic composition, accurate period setting — with 21st-century commitment to historical accuracy rather than mythological celebration. Warm afternoon light palette, deep church interior shadows, plain colonial architectural setting.
