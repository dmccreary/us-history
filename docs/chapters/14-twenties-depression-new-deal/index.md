# The Roaring Twenties, Depression, and New Deal (1920–1941)

## Summary

This chapter spans the consumer boom of the 1920s — automobile culture, the Harlem Renaissance, jazz, Prohibition, and rising nativism — through the catastrophic 1929 crash and the decade-long Great Depression, to Franklin Roosevelt's New Deal programs that created the modern social safety net (Social Security, minimum wage protections, Wagner Act) and redefined the federal government's role in American life. The Dust Bowl and the threat of fascism in Europe set the stage for U.S. entry into World War II.
