# References: Founding the Republic (1783–1800)

1. [Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_(United_States)) - Wikipedia - Covers the 1787 Philadelphia convention in detail — delegates, competing plans (Virginia vs. New Jersey), the Great Compromise, and the debates over representation and slavery.

2. [Federalism in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_the_United_States) - Wikipedia - Explains the division of powers between federal and state governments, the Anti-Federalist critique, and the ongoing tension between national authority and state sovereignty.

3. [United States Bill of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights) - Wikipedia - Traces the drafting of the first ten amendments, the political context of ratification, and the long history of their judicial interpretation and expansion.

4. Pauline Maier, *Ratification: The People Debate the Constitution, 1787–1788* (2010) - Simon & Schuster - Exhaustive, state-by-state account of the ratification debates, giving voice to the Anti-Federalists who nearly blocked the Constitution and whose objections shaped the Bill of Rights.

5. Joseph J. Ellis, *Founding Brothers: The Revolutionary Generation* (2000) - Vintage Books - Pulitzer Prize winner examining the relationships and conflicts among the Founders — Hamilton, Jefferson, Adams, Burr — that shaped the early republic's character.

6. [National Archives: Charters of Freedom](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution) - National Archives - Official transcription and high-resolution scans of the Constitution, Bill of Rights, and Declaration; includes contextual essays on ratification and amendment history.

7. [Avalon Project: The Federalist Papers](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/subject_menus/fed.asp) - Yale Law School - Complete text of all 85 Federalist Papers by Hamilton, Madison, and Jay; the primary source for understanding the constitutional architecture this chapter analyzes.

8. [Digital History: The Early Republic](https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/era.cfm?eraID=4) - University of Houston - Curated primary sources and narrative essays on Shays' Rebellion, the Constitutional Convention, and the Washington and Adams administrations.

9. [Khan Academy: A New Government](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/american-revolution-era/creating-new-government/a/articles-of-confederation) - Khan Academy - Accessible articles and videos covering the Articles of Confederation's weaknesses, the Constitutional Convention, and the Federalist/Anti-Federalist debate.

10. [Interactive Constitution — National Constitution Center](https://constitutioncenter.org/interactive-constitution) - National Constitution Center - Each clause of the Constitution is paired with essays from a liberal and a conservative scholar explaining its meaning and contested interpretations; ideal for in-depth clause analysis.
