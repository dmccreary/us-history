A somber, wide-format historical illustration (16:9 aspect ratio) depicting the Continental
Army encampment at Valley Forge, Pennsylvania during the winter of 1777–78 — styled as a
historically grounded US history textbook illustration that conveys the genuine suffering
and remarkable institutional transformation that occurred there, without the false heroism
of 19th-century mythology.

COMPOSITION — THE ENCAMPMENT AT DUSK:
The scene is set in late January or February 1778 — the worst weeks of the encampment —
at the edge of one of the hut rows, looking out across the frozen Pennsylvania landscape.
The viewpoint is from ground level, slightly elevated, capturing both the immediate human
suffering of the foreground and the vast scope of the encampment extending into the
middle distance.

THE FOREGROUND — SOLDIERS AND THEIR REALITY:
- Three to four soldiers in the immediate foreground, in various states of extreme
  want — this is the central human reality of Valley Forge and should be shown honestly:
- One soldier seated on a log outside a rough-hewn hut: his feet are wrapped in rags
  — he has no shoes; his coat is threadbare Continental Army blue, patched multiple
  times; he is trying to warm his hands over a small fire; his expression is exhausted

- Another soldier stands nearby, wrapped in a blanket over his uniform, cradling a musket;

  determination despite everything
- A third figure lies near the fire, covered in a blanket, possibly ill — disease
  (typhus, dysentery, and pneumonia) killed more soldiers at Valley Forge than combat
  did; approximately 2,000 of the 12,000 soldiers died during the winter encampment
- The ground: frozen mud with old snow; wagon ruts frozen into the earth; scattered
  evidence of the encampment's limited resources (a few wood chips from hut construction,
  a broken wagon wheel, a sparse stack of firewood)

THE HUTS:
- The log huts constructed by the soldiers themselves under Washington's orders are shown
  accurately: approximately 14 by 16 feet, built from green timber (which would have
  been drafty and smoky), mud-chinked, with rough fireplaces at one end; the roofs are
  flat or slightly pitched with boards and sod covering; each hut holds 12 men
- 12,000 soldiers built approximately 2,000 huts in a matter of weeks — an extraordinary
  collective engineering achievement; several huts visible in the mid-ground, in various
  states of completion; some men visible working on hut construction
- The huts are arranged in disciplined rows following Washington's grid-plan layout —
  this order amid chaos is itself historically significant

THE ENCAMPMENT — MIDDLE GROUND:
- Looking down the row of huts: columns of smoke rising from dozens of chimneys against
  the pale winter sky — the encampment at its full scale (12,000 soldiers plus women
  camp followers)
- A few soldiers drilling in a cleared area in the middle distance — the presence of
  Baron Friedrich von Steuben (who arrived in February 1778) and began transforming the

  drilling exercise is visible in the background
- A supply wagon arriving in the background: half-empty, which was the reality —
  the supply chain was broken, Congress was dysfunctional, and nearby farmers were
  selling food to the British (who paid in hard coin) rather than the Continental Army
