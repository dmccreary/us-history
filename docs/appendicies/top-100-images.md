# 100 Most Important Images for a U.S. History Textbook

Below is a curated chronological list of 100 iconic images that would dramatically improve visual storytelling in your U.S. History textbook. The selections are aligned with the course structure and themes in your course description and chapter outline.  

I intentionally mixed:

* famous paintings
* historic photographs
* political cartoons
* maps
* propaganda posters
* speeches
* iconic documents
* landmark moments
* symbolic monuments

These images are chosen because students often already recognize them culturally, making them powerful anchors for memory and discussion.


1. **Map of Major Indigenous Civilizations Before 1492**
   Shows the complexity and diversity of Native societies before European arrival.

2. **Cliff Palace at Mesa Verde**
   Demonstrates sophisticated Indigenous architecture and social organization.

3. **Portrait of Christopher Columbus Landing in the Americas**
   Symbolizes the beginning of sustained European contact.

4. **Map of the Columbian Exchange**
   Visualizes the transfer of plants, animals, diseases, and ideas across the Atlantic.

5. **Hernán Cortés Meeting Montezuma**
   Illustrates the collision between European empires and Indigenous civilizations.

6. **Jamestown Settlement Reconstruction**
   Represents England’s first permanent colony in North America.

7. **The Mayflower Painting**
   Symbolizes migration, religion, and the mythology of the Pilgrims.

8. **The Mayflower Compact Document**
   Early example of self-government in colonial America.

9. **The First Thanksgiving Painting**
   Useful for discussing historical myths versus evidence.

10. **Map of the Thirteen Colonies**
    Essential geographic foundation for colonial history.

11. **Slave Ship Diagram (Brookes)**
    Powerful visual of the Atlantic slave trade’s brutality.

12. **Portrait of Olaudah Equiano**
    Humanizes the experience of enslavement.

13. **Benjamin Franklin’s “Join, or Die” Cartoon**
    One of the first major American political cartoons.

14. **The Boston Massacre Engraving by Paul Revere**
    Demonstrates propaganda and revolutionary messaging.

15. **The Boston Tea Party Illustration**
    Represents colonial resistance to British taxation.

16. **The Liberty Bell**
    Enduring symbol of American independence.

17. **Patrick Henry Speaking “Give Me Liberty or Give Me Death”**
    Captures revolutionary rhetoric.

18. **George Washington Crossing the Delaware**
    One of the most iconic paintings in American history.

19. **The Declaration of Independence**
    Central founding document of American ideals.

20. **Signing of the Declaration of Independence Painting**
    Visualizes the founding generation.

21. **Winter at Valley Forge Painting**
    Symbolizes sacrifice and perseverance.

22. **Battle of Yorktown Painting**
    Represents the decisive American victory.

23. **The Articles of Confederation Document**
    Shows America’s first attempt at national government.

24. **The Constitutional Convention Scene**
    Illustrates the creation of the U.S. Constitution.

25. **The U.S. Constitution Manuscript**
    Foundational governing document.

26. **Portrait of James Madison**
    “Father of the Constitution.”

27. **The Federalist Papers Cover Page**
    Represents debates over ratification.

28. **The Bill of Rights**
    Core protections of civil liberties.

29. **George Washington Portrait (Gilbert Stuart)**
    The defining image of the first president.

30. **Louisiana Purchase Map**
    Shows the dramatic territorial expansion of the U.S.

31. **Lewis and Clark Expedition Illustration**
    Represents exploration and westward expansion.

32. **Portrait of Sacagawea**
    Highlights Indigenous contributions to exploration.

33. **Battle of New Orleans Painting**
    Symbolizes nationalism after the War of 1812.

34. **The Monroe Doctrine Cartoon or Document**
    Illustrates emerging U.S. influence in the Western Hemisphere.

35. **Andrew Jackson Portrait**
    Central figure of Jacksonian Democracy.

36. **The Trail of Tears Painting**
    Powerful depiction of forced Native removal.

37. **Map of the Oregon Trail**
    Illustrates migration and Manifest Destiny.

38. **John Gast’s “American Progress”**
    The definitive visual of Manifest Destiny ideology.

39. **The Alamo Painting**
