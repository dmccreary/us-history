## Title

U.S. History

## Audience

High school students (grades 9–11) in a standard or honors U.S. History course. Also suitable as preparation for the AP U.S. History exam.

## Prerequisites

Basic reading comprehension and general world awareness. No prior formal history coursework required.

## Topics

1. Colonial America and the Road to Independence (1607–1776)
2. The American Revolution and Founding Documents (1776–1789)
3. The Early Republic and Westward Expansion (1789–1850)
4. Sectionalism, Civil War, and Reconstruction (1850–1877)
5. Industrialization, Immigration, and the Gilded Age (1877–1900)
6. Progressivism, Imperialism, and World War I (1900–1920)
7. The Roaring Twenties and the Great Depression (1920–1940)
8. World War II and the Early Cold War (1940–1960)
9. Civil Rights, the Great Society, and Vietnam (1960–1975)
10. From Watergate to the End of the Cold War (1975–1991)
11. Globalization, 9/11, and the Modern Era (1991–present)

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this book, the reader will be able to:

- **Remember:** Identify key events, dates, figures, and documents in U.S. history from colonization to the present.
- **Understand:** Explain the causes and consequences of major historical turning points.
- **Apply:** Use primary source evidence to support historical arguments.
- **Analyze:** Compare competing historical interpretations and evaluate their evidence.
- **Evaluate:** Assess the long-term significance of political, economic, social, and cultural developments.
- **Create:** Construct evidence-based arguments about continuity and change over time in American history.
