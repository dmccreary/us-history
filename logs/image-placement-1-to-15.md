### 11 — Slave Ship Brookes
- **File:** `11-slave-ship-brookes.png`
- **Chapter:** `chapters/03-colonial-america/index.md`
- **Section:** Slavery in the Colonies / Middle Passage
- **Inserted after:** "Historians estimate that roughly 12 million people were transported across the Atlantic in the slave trade; approximately 1.5 million died during the crossing."
- **Rationale:** The Brookes diagram is one of the most effective primary-source documents in the history of the abolitionist movement precisely because it makes abstract mortality statistics viscerally concrete. Placing it immediately after the mortality sentence creates the intended juxtaposition: numbers → image → human reality. ### 15 — Boston Tea Party

- **Chapter:** `chapters/04-american-revolution/index.md`
- **Section:** The Boston Tea Party (1773) and the Intolerable Acts
- **Inserted after:** Section header `### The Boston Tea Party (1773) and the Intolerable Acts`
- **Rationale:** The Tea Party is one of the most iconically misremembered events in American history — students often know the image before they know the political logic behind it. Placing the illustration at the top of the section, before the text explains that the Sons of Liberty were protesting a *principle* (parliamentary taxation) rather than a *price* (tea was actually cheaper under the Tea Act), invites students to confront their prior mental image before the text corrects common misconceptions.
